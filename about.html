About the UGDA

The association for everyone making games in Uganda, founded in 2026 by Isaac Amumpaire.

Our story

An industry that grew up in scattered pieces

Games have been made in Uganda for more than a decade. Kola Studios, founded in 2013, turned the Ugandan card game Amatatu into Matatu, a mobile game with more than a million downloads on Google Play (Games Industry Africa, 2022). In March 2026, Game Jam 2026 brought over 40 participants together to build games in 24 hours (NBS Sport). What has been missing is anything holding those pieces together.

The Uganda Game Developers Association was founded in 2026 by Isaac Amumpaire, who works at Birungi Studio. The idea came out of a research trip sponsored by Pro Helvetia, the Swiss Arts Council: Isaac came back with a plan to build a community for Uganda’s game developers. Since then, Birungi Studio has launched its game I Bee Xploring.

Back in Uganda, Isaac met Kelele Collective, a Kampala community for multimedia creatives that had organised a meetup on game development in Uganda. Isaac and Kelele were then invited to the Nordic–Uganda Partnership Day in Kampala on 10 September 2026, hosted by the embassies of Denmark, Sweden and Iceland (The Rio Times). There, Kelele screened Ugandan games and ran a micro game pitch challenge (Kelele Collective). Birungi Studio and Kelele Collective have since partnered to push the game development community forward.

The sector has had no shared voice with government, no reliable data on its size, and no obvious path for a talented graduate to enter the industry. The UGDA is a membership association, and its priorities will be set by its members.

Our mission

Help the Ugandan games industry achieve its full potential.

We do that through four commitments:

  • Represent. One credible voice for the sector in policy rooms.
  • Connect. A community where knowledge and work circulate freely.
  • Grow. Skills, mentorship and routes into the industry.
  • Prove. Research that shows what the sector is actually worth.
Divisions

Three divisions, one association

Each division will elect its own lead and set its own agenda, while sharing the association’s resources and voice.

Entertainment

Commercial studios building games for players. Focus areas: publishing deals, funding, storefront visibility and export.

Serious games

Applied game design for health, agriculture, education and corporate training. Focus areas: procurement, impact measurement and client education.

Students & indies

Solo developers, small teams and university programmes. Focus areas: mentorship, first contracts, portfolios and affordable tooling.
