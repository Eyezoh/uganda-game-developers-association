We are here to level up the Ugandan game industry.

The Uganda Game Developers Association (UGDA) is the association for the studios, developers, artists and educators making games in Uganda — growing the sector into a serious pillar of the country’s creative economy.

About us

We’re building a tight-knit community that brings the Ugandan games industry together.

Uganda has the talent, the stories and the appetite — what it has lacked is a shared table. The UGDA exists so that studios, freelancers, publishers and schools stop working in isolation and start moving as one industry.

These are the programmes we are setting up with our first members.

01

Build the community

Regular meetups, a members’ community space and an open directory, so developers across Kampala, Gulu, Mbarara and the diaspora can find each other.

02

Connect the ecosystem

Bridges to government, telecoms, banks, investors and the wider creative sector, so that games are understood as an industry, not a hobby.

03

Grow global reputation

Ugandan delegations at international events, and press kits that put local titles in front of publishers and press.

04

Run the events

Network lunches, game jams, trade missions and, in time, an annual awards night for Ugandan games.

05

Mentor the next wave

A mentor programme that pairs emerging developers with senior practitioners for structured, practical guidance.

06

Measure the industry

Regular research on studio numbers, revenue, employment and skills gaps — the numbers advocacy needs.

Our partners

Growing game development in Uganda, together

Birungi Studio and Kelele Collective became partners after organising Kelele’s first game developers’ meetup together.

Birungi Studio

A social-impact game studio and the makers of I Bee Xploring, an educational, family-friendly game in which players collect nectar from flowers and escape the effects of urbanisation.

Kelele Collective

Membership

Open to everyone making games in Uganda

Membership is open to any company or individual making games in Uganda — from a two-person mobile studio to a university programme. Members are grouped into three divisions so that support and advocacy fit the work.

Membership will include:

  • A vote on the association’s direction and priorities
  • Discounted stands at trade shows and events
  • Access to the mentor programme and the members’ directory
  • Legal, tax and publishing-contract guidance clinics

The Ugandan scene

Game development in Uganda

Ugandan studios have been shipping games for more than a decade, and 2026 brought a government-backed national game jam.

Matatu, by Kola Studios

A mobile version of the Ugandan card game Amatatu, from a studio founded in 2013. It passed 1,000,000 downloads on Google Play, Games Industry Africa reported in May 2022.

Game Jam 2026

A 24-hour hackathon led by the Science, Technology and Innovation Secretariat with Tech Anatomy, EDIC and Arudem. Over 40 participants built games on conservation, food culture, transport and heritage.

New teams

Game Jam 2026 prizes went to Endangered by Code Chaos, Nyanya Mbisi by Loop Studio and Last Hope by Night Owls.
